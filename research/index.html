In preparation  protocol written, not yet run Results in  pilot complete, findings attached

  • Asks whether a persistent 3D world becomes more engaging when its AI residents notice, pursue and remember discoverable events. The intervention is deliberately small: a scarce, server-authoritative loot drop appears in the town, a resident may choose to investigate, the resident has to reach it through a grounded route, and a successful claim becomes a durable world event and memory.

    The argument is that the product wedge is not residents powered by a language model, which is easy to copy and hard to evaluate. It is the loop of world change, resident choice, embodied action, durable memory and human-visible history.

    What it commits to

    1. Selective exploration. Given an available drop, some residents pursue it and others carry on with routine.
    2. Grounded coherence. Narration agrees with the authoritative event ledger more often when a valid route, arrival and atomic claim are required.
    3. Durable continuity. A recorded discovery produces more accurate follow-up references than an equivalent object with no memory event.
    4. Human return value. Sessions containing a visible resident discovery raise repeat-visit measures.
    5. Scarcity constraint. Past a moderate drop frequency, perceived significance falls and behaviour converges.

    Boundary

    The synthetic resident used to make the protocol concrete is an internal case, not a recruited participant. The document uses modelled preference, activity score and recorded memory, and does not claim that any resident feels curiosity or wants a reward. That is a design constraint, not cautious wording: it keeps generated self-report from being mistaken for evidence of inner experience.
